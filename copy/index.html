JOSH: If ten new clients paid you next Monday, who would onboard them?

Not who should. Who actually would.

If the honest answer is "me," or "me, after I check Stripe and my DMs," then this video is for you. Because that answer means your business has outgrown the duct tape holding it together. And the thing it is taped to is you.

0:25 Who this is for

ON SCREEN: Proven offer. Real results. Consistent demand.

JOSH: I'm Josh, founder of Keep It Simple. We build KIS OS for established health and fitness businesses. Online coaches, gym owners, nutrition brands. People with a proven offer, real client results and consistent demand, collecting about $30K a month or more.

If you are just starting out, this is not your next step yet, and I'll tell you what is at the end.

0:50 The problem nobody warns you about

ON SCREEN: More demand does not fix the business. It exposes it.

JOSH: Here is what nobody tells you when the marketing starts working. More demand does not fix the business. It exposes it.

Leads, follow-up, agreements, payments, onboarding, Trainerize, team responsibilities, retention and reporting all live in separate tools and manual handoffs. And who connects them? You do. With your memory, your messages, your spreadsheets and your personal follow-through.

So you get the record month. And then you spend the next month delivering it instead of selling.

1:40 The question to ask yourself

ON SCREEN: How many of this month's new clients will still be here in 90 days?

JOSH: Here is a question worth sitting with. If you added twenty clients this month, how many of them would still be with you in ninety days?

[JOSH TO CONFIRM: if Josh wants to tell his "gain 20, lose 16" observation here, it must be framed as what he has personally seen with coaches he talks to, not as a statistic. Leave it out if he is not comfortable stating it on camera.]

Most founders think they have a lead problem. A lot of the time the system behind the sale is where the clients leak out.

2:15 Why the usual fixes don't work

ON SCREEN: Another login does not fix the handoffs between your tools.

JOSH: So what do most of us do? We buy another tool. Then another. A scheduling app, a forms app, a Zapier chain, a payment tool, a coaching app. Each one solves a piece.

But another login does not fix the handoffs between your tools. Tools solve pieces. Somebody still has to own the handoffs between them.

Or we hire. A VA, an ops person, an assistant coach. Good people. But if the system is broken, you are hiring people to fix a systems problem, and now they are asking you the questions instead.

3:00 What KIS OS is

ON SCREEN: One sale. Eight coordinated actions. No founder handoffs.

JOSH: KIS OS is different. It is not another app. It is one connected operating system we install around your business, with one accountable team owning the handoffs.

Here is what one sale looks like inside it.

B-ROLL: [SCREEN RECORDING: the real "one sale in motion" flow in a live KIS OS build, no client data]

The sale is confirmed and the payment fires. Onboarding starts and intake goes out. Trainerize delivery is created. The coach and the team are assigned. The Command Center updates. A coach begins the client relationship. Eligible next-step opportunities get flagged for the team [JOSH TO CONFIRM wording; this is the KIS Rx step, kept non-clinical]. Retention signals stay visible across the journey.

One sale. Eight coordinated actions. No founder handoffs.

3:50 Who owns what

ON SCREEN: The system handles. The team owns. You lead.

JOSH: That changes who does what.

The system handles capture and routing, reminders, status updates, automated onboarding and reporting prep.

Your team owns sales follow-up, onboarding completion, coaching, client attention and retention follow-through.

And you lead. Priorities, people, offers, partnerships, growth decisions. Only the decisions the founder should make.

The business moves. The team owns it. You lead it.

4:30 Keep what works

ON SCREEN: Keep. Connect. Improve. Migrate.

JOSH: And no, we do not rip out everything you have built. No reckless rip-and-replace. No login dump.

Every tool and workflow you run today gets one of four decisions. Keep it. Connect it. Improve it. Or migrate it. We protect what works and replace what doesn't.

5:00 How it gets installed

ON SCREEN: Map. Install. Test and launch. Operate and grow.

JOSH: The install has four steps.

One, we map the constraint: where growth is breaking and what KIS OS should install first.

Two, we install KIS OS. We architect, build, connect and configure the agreed system.

Three, we test and launch. The critical handoffs get tested, ownership gets confirmed, and the system moves into daily use.

Four, you operate and grow. Your team owns defined work, the Command Center surfaces the exceptions, and you lead the next stage.

Most installs are planned around approximately eight weeks. The exact timing depends on your scope, access and integrations, and we confirm it in writing before you commit.

6:10 What happens on the call

ON SCREEN: Book a KIS OS demo.

JOSH: If this sounds like your business, the next step is a demo call with our team.

On the call we look at your actual business. We find your primary operating constraint, take a snapshot of where things still route back to you, give you a keep, connect, improve or migrate direction on your current tools, and a now, next and later sequence.

And you get a candid fit decision. If KIS OS is not the right next step, we will say so and tell you what should come first.

ON SCREEN: Book your demo. (button below the video)

JOSH: You built the demand. Let us build the operating system around it.

Let's make the answer "the system."
